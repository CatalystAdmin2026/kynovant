# Catalyst OS — Voice and Tone

Established 2026-07-23

---

## What This Document Is

This is the product language standard for Catalyst OS. It applies to every word in the product — UI copy, notifications, emails, empty states, error messages, onboarding, success states, AI-generated drafts, coach-facing tools, and client-facing surfaces.

It is not a marketing document. It does not describe how to sell Catalyst. It describes how Catalyst should sound when it is already in the hands of a coach or a client who has chosen it.

Consistency of voice is a product quality issue. A product that sounds like one person wrote the client portal, a different person wrote the HQ dashboard, and a third person wrote the notifications will feel assembled rather than designed. Clients and coaches feel this even when they cannot name it. The result is reduced trust.

Any new screen, feature, or communication added to Catalyst should be reviewed against this document before implementation is considered complete.

---

## The Standard

**Catalyst should sound like an exceptional coach.**

Not software. Not a corporate HR system. Not a motivational speaker, a fitness influencer, or a military drill instructor. An exceptional coach — the kind of person who tells clients the truth, notices what matters, and earns trust by being consistently useful over time.

When evaluating any piece of copy, apply this test:

> Would an exceptional coach say this to a client they respect?

If the answer is no, the copy is wrong — regardless of whether it is technically accurate, grammatically correct, or common in other software products.

## Voice

These are stable characteristics. Catalyst's voice does not change based on context or platform. The tone adjusts (see below), but the underlying character does not.

### Catalyst is:

**Calm.** Never urgent unless urgency is genuinely warranted. Does not raise the client's anxiety to motivate them. Doesn't need to.

**Confident.** States things directly. Does not hedge every sentence with "might," "maybe," or "it seems like." When the data is clear, says so clearly.

**Direct.** Uses the minimum number of words to say something precisely. Does not pad copy with filler phrases, corporate hedges, or unnecessary warmth signaling.

**Honest.** Does not invent success. Does not soften hard truths into meaninglessness. Does not present partial progress as complete victory.

**Supportive.** Present in difficulty without rescuing the client from it. Reinforces capability rather than creating dependency. Support sounds like: "You have what it takes to work through this." Not: "Don't worry, everything will be fine."

**Premium.** Language is precise and intentional. Nothing sounds cheap, rushed, or like it was written to fill a wireframe. Every sentence was chosen.

**Human.** Reads like a person wrote it. Not a content management system. Contractions are fine. Incomplete sentences are fine if they serve rhythm.

**Professional.** Does not become casual to the point of being careless. Maintains respect for the client's time and intelligence.

**Respectful.** Treats the client as an adult who can handle the truth and make their own decisions.

**Thoughtful.** The question "How will this make the client feel?" is asked before every piece of copy ships.

---

### Catalyst is not:

**Loud.** Does not use exclamation points as a substitute for meaning.

**Overly excited.** Does not celebrate the ordinary. Does not use "Amazing!" or "Incredible!" for routine actions.

**Sarcastic.** Never. There is no context in which sarcasm is appropriate in a coaching product.

**Condescending.** Does not explain things the client already knows. Does not treat adults like they need to be managed.

**Guilt-driven.** Does not use guilt, shame, or social comparison to motivate action. Does not imply the client has disappointed anyone.

**Aggressive.** Does not use confrontational, urgent, or combat-oriented language — "crush," "destroy," "beast mode," "grind."

**Salesy.** Does not use persuasion techniques to get clients to take actions they might not otherwise take. The product earns engagement; it does not engineer it.

**Cheesy.** Does not use clichés, sports metaphors, or generic inspirational language. "You've got this!" is cheesy. "You've done this before" is not.

**Generic.** Specific is always better than general. "You completed every planned workout this week" is always better than "Great job!"

**Artificially inspirational.** Does not produce language that sounds meaningful but means nothing. "Every day is a new opportunity to be your best self" belongs in a motivational poster, not a coaching platform.

## Tone

Voice is who Catalyst is. Tone is how that voice adapts to the context.

The same person can speak with warmth and encouragement in one moment and quiet directness in another — without becoming a different person. That is how Catalyst's tone works.

| Context | Tone adjustment |
|---|---|
| First check-in, onboarding | Warm, welcoming, patient |
| Available check-in window | Inviting, calm, low-pressure |
| Overdue check-in | Honest, non-punitive, still inviting |
| Draft state | Practical, matter-of-fact |
| Check-in submitted | Settled, acknowledging |
| Coach feedback ready | Engaged, forward-looking |
| First week of program | Grounding, orienting |
| Difficult week acknowledged | Honest, sturdy, not pitying |
| Milestone earned | Warm but understated — earned, not performed |
| Error / something went wrong | Steady, reassuring, practical |
| Empty state | Intentional, not apologetic |
| Coach-facing UI | Efficient, clinical-in-the-best-sense, respects the coach's time |

Tone adjusts. The underlying voice does not.

## Emotional Philosophy

Every piece of copy has an emotional effect, whether intended or not. In Catalyst, that effect is a product decision.

Before writing any copy, ask: **How should the reader feel after reading this?**

### Preferred emotional outcomes:

- **Understood** — the product has seen their situation accurately
- **Recognized** — their effort has been acknowledged specifically and honestly
- **Supported** — someone is working on their behalf
- **Capable** — they have what it takes
- **Encouraged** — the next step is available and accessible
- **Responsible** — their actions matter and are recorded with care
- **Hopeful** — forward motion is possible from where they are right now
- **Respected** — they are treated as an intelligent adult

### Emotional states to avoid causing:

- **Guilt** — "Your coach is waiting." "You're behind."
- **Shame** — "You missed three workouts." "That's not good enough."
- **Embarrassment** — Nothing. There is nothing in coaching data that should be used to embarrass a client.
- **Panic** — False urgency, countdown timers, red banners for non-emergencies.
- **Pressure** — Persuasion through obligation, social comparison, or disapproval.
- **Artificial urgency** — "Limited time!" "Act now!" "Your coach is waiting!" applied to routine check-ins.

## Writing Principles

These are numbered for reference, not priority. All of them apply at all times.

---

### 1. Recognition before reporting

When presenting behavioral data, recognize the effort before presenting the number.

The number is evidence. The recognition is the coaching.

A client who opened this page after a difficult week needs to feel seen before they feel measured.

❌ **Wrong:**
> 31% toward your goal.

✅ **Right:**
> You kept every promise you made to yourself this week.
>
> 31% toward your goal.

---

### 2. Invite reflection. Do not issue tasks.

When asking a client to do something, frame it as an invitation to reflect rather than a task to complete.

A check-in is not homework. It is an opportunity to give the coach an accurate picture of where the client is so they can be coached more effectively.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Complete your weekly check-in.

✅ **Right:**
> How did this week go?
>
> Your coach reads every check-in and uses it to guide what comes next.

---

### 3. The client is the hero. The coach is the guide.

The client is doing the work. The coach supports the journey. The product supports the coaching relationship.

None of the three should be centered on any screen in a way that diminishes the others, but when in doubt: write toward the client's perspective.

A screen that begins "Your coach is waiting" has made the coach the main character and turned the client into a supporting role — someone who exists to complete tasks for someone else.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Your coach is waiting.

✅ **Right:**
> How did this week go?
>
> Your coach reads every check-in and uses it to guide what comes next.

---

### 4. Celebrate honestly. Never fabricate.

Praise must always be supported by data. Never praise the ordinary as extraordinary. Never invent positive framing where the data does not support it.

A client who reads "You're crushing it!" after a week where they missed three workouts will not trust the next piece of recognition they receive.

Specific recognition is more valuable than enthusiastic recognition. "You completed every planned workout this week" carries more weight than any amount of exclamation points.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Amazing job! Keep it up!

✅ **Right:**
> You completed every planned workout this week.

When there is no win to recognize, say something true and useful instead of manufacturing a compliment.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Even not-so-great weeks build character!

✅ **Right:**
> One difficult week does not erase your progress. Your next opportunity starts now.

---

### 5. Be specific. Always.

Generic encouragement is the cheapest form of recognition. It signals that the product does not know the client.

Specific recognition — even if it comes from a template — creates the feeling of being seen.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Great work!

✅ **Right:**
> You completed 3 of 4 planned sessions this week.

❌ **Wrong:**
> You're making progress!

✅ **Right:**
> You're 31% toward your goal — down 6.6 lbs since you started.

The data that makes specificity possible is the reason the check-in system exists. Use it.

---

### 6. Treat honesty as success

The product should actively reinforce that honest reporting — including reporting that things did not go well — is valuable.

This is true in behavioral design terms: a client who reports a missed workout honestly is more coachable than one who avoids the check-in entirely. The product should make the honest choice the easy and rewarded choice.

On the submission success experience:
> Thank you for being honest. The more honest your check-ins are, the better your coach can help you.

On the overdue check-in:
> An honest update — even if the week did not go as planned — gives your coach what they need to help you move forward.

Do not reward perfection. Reward engagement and honesty.

## Language Rules

### Words that belong in Catalyst:

- reflect, reflection
- guide, guidance
- progress
- consistency
- momentum
- support, supportive
- coaching, coach
- next step, next week
- follow-through
- improve, improvement
- committed, commitment
- honest, honesty
- record, recognize
- build, building
- ready
- capable

### Words that do not belong in Catalyst:

- crush, crushing it
- dominate, domination
- beast, beast mode
- hustle, grind
- failure (prefer: difficult week, setback, off week)
- lazy (never)
- excuses (never)
- punishment (never)
- cheat meal (never — it frames honesty as transgression)
- amazing! incredible! fantastic! (empty superlatives)
- action required (unless the consequence is actually time-sensitive and real)
- task (clients are not completing tasks; they are reflecting on their week)
- user (clients are clients, not users)
- crushing your goals (clients are working toward their goals, not crushing them)
- just (as in "just click here" — it minimizes the reader's effort)

## Surface-by-Surface Standards

---

### Success states

Success should feel earned. Not performed.

The correct response to a completed check-in, a submitted form, or a finished session is not celebration — it is acknowledgement. The client did what they came to do. The product should confirm it, not erupt.

✅ **Right:**
> Your coach has everything they need.
>
> Thank you for being honest.
>
> The more honest your check-ins are, the better your coach can help you.
>
> Your coach will review this and respond as soon as possible.

❌ **Wrong:**
> 🎉 Success!
>
> Great job!! Mission accomplished!

There are no exclamation points in Catalyst success states.

---

### Empty states

Empty states should feel intentional — as if the space is waiting purposefully, not as if something is broken or absent.

They should explain why the space is empty, what will appear there, and when — without apology, and without filler.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Nothing here yet.

❌ **Wrong:**
> No data available.

✅ **Right:**
> No resources shared yet.
>
> All coaching materials will appear here as your program progresses. Your library typically begins building in the first week of training.

✅ **Right:**
> Your check-in history will appear here after your first submission.

✅ **Right:**
> Your first check-in starts your coaching story.

Empty states that describe what is coming are better than empty states that describe what is absent.

---

### Error states

Errors should never blame the client.

The client did not do something wrong. Something went wrong in the system. That is a factual distinction, and it matters to trust.

When something goes wrong, the error state should:
1. State what happened plainly
2. Reassure the client that their work or progress is safe (when true)
3. Give them a next step

❌ **Wrong:**
> Invalid submission. Please correct the errors and try again.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Error: Something went wrong.

✅ **Right:**
> Something went wrong while saving your check-in.
>
> Your progress hasn't been lost. Please try again.

If data may have been lost, say so plainly rather than offering false reassurance. "We were unable to save your last response. Please re-enter it before submitting." is honest. "Your data is safe!" when it may not be, is not.

---

### Loading states

Loading states should feel like something meaningful is being prepared — not like a machine is working on an unknown task.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Loading...

✅ **Right:**
> Preparing your program.

✅ **Right:**
> Loading your check-in history.

When the loading time is expected to be brief, no loading copy is necessary — a visual indicator is sufficient. Loading copy is most valuable when the operation takes more than 1–2 seconds.

---

### Notifications

Notifications should communicate value, not action obligations.

Every notification should answer: **what does the client now have access to?** Not: **what does the client need to do?**

❌ **Wrong:**
> Action required.
> Coach responded.
> Task complete.
> Check-in overdue.

✅ **Right:**
> Your latest coaching is ready.
> Your program has been updated.
> A new resource is available in your library.

Notifications that communicate urgency should be reserved for things that are actually urgent. Do not use urgency language for routine product updates. A client who receives too many "action required" notifications will start treating all of them as ambient noise.

Push notifications (future) follow the same standard. If the notification cannot be written in a way that communicates genuine value to the client, it should not be sent.

---

### Onboarding

Onboarding copy is the first impression of Catalyst's voice. It establishes what this coaching relationship will feel like.

Onboarding should feel like the beginning of something — not a registration process.

Use language that orients rather than instructs. Tell the client what they are building, not what they are filling out.

❌ **Wrong:**
> Please complete all required fields.

✅ **Right:**
> Your coach will use this information to build your first program. Be as honest as you can — the more accurate this is, the better your starting point.

Onboarding should not congratulate the client for completing forms. Completing a form is not an achievement. Beginning a coaching program is.

---

### Coach-facing UI

The coach-facing interface (HQ) is professional, efficient, and dense. Coaches are experts. They do not need to be coaxed or encouraged. They need accurate information presented clearly and tools that work without ceremony.

Coach-facing copy should be:
- More concise than client-facing copy
- Less warm in tone, not less respectful
- Clearly labeled with operational language (Review, Assign, Archive, Revoke)
- Free of the softening language used in client surfaces

A coach view should feel like a professional tool. A client view should feel like a coaching relationship.

That said, the underlying voice principles apply to coaches too. Error messages in HQ should not blame the coach. Empty states in HQ should be informative and purposeful. Nothing should feel cheap.

---

### AI-generated content

AI-generated drafts in Catalyst — narrative summaries, check-in response drafts, progress interpretations — must follow the same voice standard as hand-written copy.

An AI-generated response that sounds robotic, overly enthusiastic, or unlike the rest of the product is not acceptable even if the coach chooses to approve it. The AI's role is to amplify the coach's voice — not to replace it with a different voice.

Guidelines for AI-generated content:

**The AI should describe. The coach should interpret.** AI can say: "Session completion declined 30% over the last three weeks." The coach determines why and what to do about it.

**The AI should not perform enthusiasm.** AI-generated narratives should not open with "What an incredible week!" or any equivalent. Enthusiasm that is not grounded in data is noise.

**The AI should not invent wins.** If the week was genuinely difficult, the AI draft should say something truthful about it — not manufacture positive framing to protect the client's feelings. The coach can add nuance in their review. The AI should not start that review process with a false premise.

**The AI's voice is Catalyst's voice.** A client should not be able to tell the difference between copy written by a person and copy written by the AI and approved by a coach. Both should sound like the same coaching system.

See also: `AI_PRINCIPLES.md` for the full behavioral standard governing AI in Catalyst.

## Examples

The following table documents past decisions and the rationale behind them. Use it as a reference when evaluating new copy.

| ❌ Old wording | ✅ Improved wording | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your coach is waiting. | How did this week go? | Centers the client. Removes guilt. Reframes check-in as reflection, not obligation. |
| Complete your check-in. | Take a few minutes to reflect on your week. | Invitation over instruction. Gives the action meaning. |
| Great job! | You completed every planned workout this week. | Specific over generic. Honest and verifiable. |
| Reviewed. | Feedback Ready. | Communicates value to the client, not internal status. |
| Save & Exit. | Pick up where you left off. | Continuity framing. Removes anxiety about leaving mid-form. |
| Almost there. | Pick up where you left off. | Removes vague encouragement. States the actual situation clearly. |
| Check-in received. | Your check-in is ready for your coach. | Client-perspective framing. Tells them what their action enabled. |
| Your coach left feedback. | Your latest coaching is ready. | The coaching is the value, not the coach's action. |
| Loading... | Preparing your program. | Purposeful over mechanical. |
| No data yet. | Your check-in history will appear here after your first submission. | Explains the future rather than naming the absence. |
| Error: Invalid submission. | Something went wrong while saving your check-in. Your progress hasn't been lost. | No blame. Reassurance. Next step. |
| Action required. | Your latest coaching is ready. | Value over obligation. |
| Coach responded. | Your latest coaching is ready. | Same correction — notifies value, not event. |
| You missed your check-in. | You can still check in. | Eliminates accusation. Keeps the door open. |
| Overdue. (as headline) | You can still check in. (with Overdue as date detail) | Status is context, not the lead. |

## The Test

When evaluating any piece of copy in Catalyst, apply the following test:

**1. Would an exceptional coach say this to a client they respect?**
If no: rewrite.

**2. Does this copy tell the client what they *have* access to, rather than what they *need to do*?**
If it tells them what to do without explaining the value: rewrite.

**3. Is the praise specific and data-supported?**
If the praise is generic or cannot be traced to an actual data point: rewrite.

**4. Does this copy create guilt, shame, or artificial urgency?**
If yes: rewrite.

**5. Does this copy sound like the same product that wrote everything else?**
If it sounds like a different product or a different era of the product: rewrite.

## Future Requirement

Any new feature added to Catalyst must be reviewed against this document before implementation is considered complete.

This means:

- Every screen has gone through the voice test
- Every empty state is intentional and explanatory
- Every error state does not blame the client
- Every success state is honest and proportional
- Every notification communicates value
- Every piece of AI-generated content follows the same standard as human-written content

A feature that functions correctly but sounds wrong is not complete. Voice and function are both product quality. One does not substitute for the other.

Every screen should feel like it belongs to the same coaching system.
